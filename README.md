# infonium

infonium digests what you read, watch and listen to in your language, in the style you choose: a summary with takeaways, a guide, a news flash, a brief, a paper's reading, an outline, questions and answers, or a digest you describe yourself. Every statement links back to the passage of the source that supports it. One click takes you to the sentence in the article, the lines on the PDF page, or the moment in the recording. It also translates a source into your language in the source's own form, and answers questions about one source or about everything you have read, with the same links. You mark passages and write notes that link back to them, and both outlive a second reading of their source.

## What it reads

| Source | Add it by | Read through |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Web article | pasting its address, or uploading the page saved from your browser (for a site that turns infonium away) | the page's HTML, and the page as a browser builds it when its HTML holds little text |
| PDF, with a text layer or scanned | uploading the file or pasting its address | [MinerU](https://github.com/opendatalab/MinerU), which also recognises the text of scanned pages |
| Video or podcast page, such as YouTube, Bilibili, TED, Apple Podcasts and SoundCloud | pasting its address | the page's own captions, or speech recognition when it has none |
| Audio or video file | uploading the file or pasting its address | speech recognition |

A PDF opens as its pages, with the text read from them one tab away. A web article opens as its text, with the page it was read from one tab away. A recording opens as a player above its transcript. A PDF read for the first time shows its pages as soon as the file has arrived, while its text is still being read; the text, highlights, translation and questions come when the reading ends, and the pages then open where the reader stood.

## What it does with a source

A column beside the reader holds three tabs: the digest (摘要), a chat (对话), and a notebook (笔记). The citations of a digest are numbered from 1 through the whole digest, and those of an answer through the answer, in the order their passages are first cited; a passage cited again keeps its number. Under its tabs, 摘要和对话的模型 chooses the model that writes a digest, answers a question and translates one block again (↻), among the same models as 翻译模型 below. A digest that is kept stays when another model is chosen, until it is written again (重新生成).

### Digests

Nothing is written about a source until you ask for it. Under 摘要 you choose a style, and the model chosen under 摘要和对话的模型 writes the source's digest in it. With no digest written, the tab lists the styles with what each holds, and pressing one writes it; 自定义 opens a box for your own instructions first. Once a digest is written, a row of every style stands above the one on show, and a dot marks each style written. A style written already is shown again without asking the model; one not written yet shows what it holds and 生成. A source keeps one digest of each style, and writing a style again (重新生成) replaces only that style's digest. The tab opens on the style you chose last, where the source has it or it is being written, and otherwise on the one written last. A digest still being written when you come back to its source shows as being written, and appears when it is done. The browser remembers the style chosen last and the custom instructions written last, as it remembers the models chosen.

| Style | What its digest holds | Drawn from |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 概述与要点 | A summary of three to five sentences, then five to ten takeaways: the key claims, findings, numbers, definitions, recommendations and caveats. | infonium's first digest. Every digest kept before there were styles is one of these. |
| 导读 | One paragraph on what the source is, who wrote or speaks in it, its main argument or findings and where it ends up; then three to six key topics in the order the source takes them up, each opening with the topic's name in bold. | The source guide of Gemini Notebook (NotebookLM until 2026-07-16), which holds a summary and key topics. |
| 快讯 | One paragraph of one to three sentences under the heading 快讯, the most important first. The lead says what happened, who did it, when and where, calling people and organisations by the names the source gives them, or, of a source that reports no event, its main finding, claim or advice. Each further sentence adds one key number, consequence or next step. | The news flash of the wire services, and the inverted pyramid of news writing, whose lead puts who, what, when and where first and the details after it in falling order of importance. |
| 简报 | The bottom line in one sentence a reader could stop after, why it matters, three to six key facts, and, where the source gives them, its caveats (yes, but) and what comes next. | Axios's Smart Brevity, whose items run "What's new", "Why it matters", "The big picture", "Yes, but" and "What's next", and BLUF, bottom line up front. |
| 论文精读 | The work in one sentence, then its problem and motivation, its method, its results with the numbers, datasets, baselines and measures it reports, and the limitations it states itself. Of a source that is no paper, the parts that apply. | S. Keshav's "How to Read a Paper", whose first pass answers five Cs, and Fabric's `summarize_paper` pattern ([github.com/danielmiessler/fabric](https://github.com/danielmiessler/fabric)). |
| 章节速览 | Three to ten sections that follow the source's own order, each titled by its heading, or, in a recording or a text without headings, by its topic, with one to four points each. A citation opens the passage, and in a recording the moment it is said. | The 章节速览 of 通义听悟 (Alibaba Tingwu), whose summaries of a recording are 全文摘要, 章节速览, 发言总结 and 问答回顾. |
| 问答 | Four to eight questions a reader of the source would ask, from the broadest to the most specific, each answered from the source in a short paragraph. Only questions the source answers are asked. | The FAQ among Gemini Notebook's reports (briefing doc, study guide, FAQ, timeline and "create your own"), and Tingwu's 问答回顾. |
| 自定义 | The digest you describe, in up to 2,000 characters: its structure, its length, its tone and what it dwells on. | Gemini Notebook's "create your own" report. |

Every style keeps the same rules:

- Each statement rests on one to three quotes of the source, and states only what the source says, with no outside knowledge and no opinion of the model's.
- Each statement says one thing, and no two statements of a digest say the same thing.
- A statement says directly what the source says, not "the article says"; it names who holds a view, the author, a speaker or a party, only where that matters: for an opinion, a disputed claim, or one of several voices.
- The names, numbers, dates and units of the source are kept, and the one or two words a reader skimming the digest most needs, a key term, a name or a number, are set in bold.
- A section is a list, its statements one under another, or a paragraph, its statements run on; each statement is followed by its citations, and its ✎ puts it into a note.
- Within a style's ranges a source gets as many statements as its length and substance carry, and a section that the source gives nothing for is left out.
- The digest is written in the language of digests, `INFONIUM_LANGUAGE`, and punctuated as that language is written.

The rules against repetition come from Fabric's `summarize` and `extract_wisdom` patterns. Those on names and numbers come from Adams et al. 2023, "From Sparse to Dense: GPT-4 Summarization with Chain of Density Prompting": its readers preferred summaries about as dense in names and numbers as those people write.

A custom digest follows your instructions in its structure, length, tone and focus, and holds to the rules above whatever they say: its statements still cite the source and say only what it says, in the language of digests and its punctuation. The instructions are shown above the digest, with 修改要求 to change them and write the digest again. The box is filled with the instructions of the source's custom digest, or else with those you wrote last in this browser, and counts their characters against the limit. When a writing fails, the box keeps the instructions it was given, and text not yet sent stays in the box while you look at another style.

### Translation

A switch above the reader shows the source (原文), its translation (译文), or both (对照). A translation is made when a source is first shown with the switch on 译文 or 对照, into the language of digests, and arrives block by block. It keeps the form of its source:

| Source | Its translation |
| --- | --- |
| PDF | On the page: each block is drawn where its lines stand, in the page's layout and at the size of its text. With both on show and room for two, the source's page stands beside the translated page. The translation carries the page's links where it keeps their words. |
| Recording | As lines that follow the playback: each sentence of the transcript with its translation. A click on a line plays from that moment. |
| Web article | In the page's own layout: each block's translation stands in the place of its words, or after them with both on show. |

Every source can also be read as text, each block in translation or followed by its translation. A formula is set as a formula and is never translated. One block can be translated again, by the model that writes digests.

Beside the switch, 翻译模型 chooses the model a translation is made with: the translating model of the settings, the model that writes digests, or one of GLM's models, reached at GLM's endpoint with GLM's key. Like the switch, the choice keeps its place from one source to the next. It applies to a translation that starts, goes on after it stopped, or starts again (全部重新翻译); a translation under way keeps its model, also after a restart. The line above the text names the model that translates or translated the source. A GLM model is offered without GLM's key set, but cannot be chosen.

### Zoom

Beside the views, − 100% + makes what the reader shows smaller or larger, apart from the rest of the window: a PDF's pages by their width, and the text as a whole. The two keep a zoom each, from one source to the next; pressing the percentage returns to 100%. Pinching on a touchpad over the pages or the text zooms them as far as the fingers go, and so does holding Ctrl while turning the wheel, a notch by about a fifth; the pages zoom around the place under the pointer. Pages zoomed wider than the column scroll sideways inside it. Once the zoom stands still, a page zoomed in past the drawing it has is drawn again at its new size, so that its print stays sharp, and a translation over the pages is set again for their new width, as on a page made narrower or wider.

### Links in a PDF

The links of a PDF's pages work as in a PDF reader. One to a place of the paper, as a citation, a figure, a table or a section, turns the pages there, and ↩ at the foot of the pages returns to where you were. One to an address on the web opens it in a new tab. A link shows when the pointer is on it. Over a translation set on the page, the translation's words carry the links: a citation, a year or an address where the translation keeps them, and a label such as "Figure 3" where it gives it as 图 3.

### The chat

The chat answers a question about the open source, or about the whole library (整个库). An answer appears as it is written. Each of its statements carries citations that open the quoted passage, in whichever source it lies.

### The notebook

The notebook holds your highlights (高亮) and your notes (笔记). It can be opened with a source on show or with none.

A highlight is a passage you marked, with a comment if you wrote one. Select words in a source and choose 高亮, 批注 (mark and comment) or 记到笔记 (put the passage into a note). A passage may run over several paragraphs. You can select in every view:

| View | How a passage is selected |
| --- | --- |
| The text of any source, and a recording's lines | By selecting its words. With only a translation on show, the selection marks the whole sentences or paragraphs it touches. |
| A PDF's pages | By dragging over the lines. Over a translation set on the page, by selecting its text, which marks the whole blocks it touches. |
| A web article's own page | By selecting its words in the page. |

A highlight is drawn in every view of its source. Pressing one opens it in the notebook, and pressing its words in the notebook shows the passage in the source. With no source open, the notebook lists the highlights of every source.

A note is formatted text that belongs to the library, not to one source. A link in a note may lead to a passage of any source, and pressing it opens the passage. A statement of a digest, a statement or a whole answer of the chat, a highlight and a selection can each be put into the note being written (记到笔记), with their citations as such links. A highlight goes in with its comment, written as plain text after the quotation and its link; a comment still being typed is kept first. A web address in a statement, a comment or a quotation becomes a link in the note: one that starts with http://, https:// or www., without the punctuation that ends the sentence after it or a bracket that closes before it. An address of this machine stays text. Beside a statement the control is the ✎ that shows when the statement is pointed at. A note is kept by itself a moment after you stop typing, and a new note is kept once something is written in it. The note open in a tab is open again when the tab is loaded again.

### Reading a source again

The reader's head offers to read a source again (重新读取): a link is fetched again, and an upload is read again from the file infonium keeps. A reading that gives other text than the readings before it is kept as a new revision, and every earlier revision stays. The head lists the revisions, and an earlier one opens as its text.

Your highlights, the passages your notes link to and the citations of earlier answers are looked for in the new reading. A passage that is found is shown where it stands now; where its words differ a little, the notebook says so. A passage the new reading no longer holds is said to be lost, with the revision it was marked in, and opens in that revision.

A digest belongs to the reading it was written from. A source read again into other text has no digest until you choose a style for it again; one read again into the text it held before has the digests of that text.

## Run it

You need [uv](https://docs.astral.sh/uv/), Node.js 22 or later, [pnpm](https://pnpm.io/), and an API key for an OpenAI-compatible model.

```bash
cp .env.example .env    # put your API key in .env
make install
make dev
```

Open <http://localhost:5173>. Paste an address or upload a file. The source opens in the reader; under 摘要 beside it, choose a style, and its digest is written there. Click a numbered citation to jump to the quoted passage. Switch to 译文 to read the source in your language, and ask about it under 对话.

The API listens on port 8710 and the UI on 5173. When one is taken, pass another: `make dev API_PORT=8720 UI_PORT=5180`.

Some sources and features need one more program:

| For | Install |
| --- | --- |
| Web pages that scripts build | [Google Chrome](https://www.google.com/chrome/), as the `google-chrome` command, or Chromium, on Linux 5.9 or later; `playwright install` is not needed. The browser must start with its sandbox, which infonium checks before each page and never turns off: on Ubuntu 23.10 and later only a packaged Chrome with an AppArmor profile does, and in a container the seccomp profile must allow user namespaces (not tried). A page whose HTML holds little text is opened in it and read again. Without it, such a page is read from its HTML alone, and one with no text there is not read. Set `INFONIUM_BROWSER` when the command is another or not on the `PATH`. |
| PDFs | [MinerU](https://github.com/opendatalab/MinerU) 3.4.2 with its `core` extra (`mineru[core]==3.4.2`), as the `mineru` command. infonium reads what this version writes, and with another version every PDF fails. Set `INFONIUM_PDF_ENGINE` when the command is not on the `PATH`. |
| Recordings | [FFmpeg](https://ffmpeg.org/), as the `ffmpeg` and `ffprobe` commands on the `PATH`. |
| Recordings without captions | A speech recogniser. [`engines/speech`](engines/speech) starts one on your machine with two commands. |
| Passages found by their meaning, for questions about the whole library | An embedding engine. [`engines/embedding`](engines/embedding) starts one on your machine, on the CPU, with one command. Without it, the library is searched by words alone. |

The Python that infonium runs on must be able to load SQLite extensions: infonium loads [sqlite-vec](https://github.com/asg017/sqlite-vec) into every connection to the library's file. uv's Python 3.13 can. On a Python whose `sqlite3` cannot, infonium does not start; this comes from reading the code and has not been tried.

## How it works

A source is read into a document: an ordered list of blocks such as headings, paragraphs and tables. Every block of a recording, and every block of a PDF but a figure, also knows its places in the source. In a PDF, a place is a box on a page that holds part of the block, usually one line. In a recording, a place is the stretch of time in which part of the block is spoken.

The model reads the numbered blocks and must back every statement with a quote from one of them. infonium then looks each quote up in the document and keeps only the citations it finds there, as anchors: a block plus a character range.

A digest's instructions are the same for every style but for one part. They explain the blocks, the language and its punctuation, the citing and the writing rules of [Digests](#digests), and then give the style's recipe: which sections to write, in which form, with how many statements and what each holds. A custom digest has the reader's instructions in place of a recipe, given as they were written, under the rules. The recipes are data, kept in `src/infonium/recipe.py`, each with its name and the description a reader is offered. The model's draft gives each section its title, its form and its statements. A document's digests are kept in the library's file, one of each style, with the instructions of a custom one. A library kept before there were styles opens with each of its digests as a 概述与要点, every section a list.

The reader resolves an anchor to that range, brings it into view and makes it glow for 1.4 seconds, fading over half a second more. In a PDF's pages the glow lies on the words of the range, cut out of the lines that hold them. Where only a block's translation is on show, in the text or set over a page, the glow lies on the sentences of the translation that stand for the sentences the range runs through: a block's translation is given whole, so the sentences of the block and of its translation are paired in their order, one for one where the two have as many and by their lengths where they do not. In a recording it plays from the place where the range is spoken.

infonium keeps a snapshot of every source it reads: the HTML of a page, the PDF file, or the sound of a recording. The reader shows the snapshot, so a citation still leads to its passage after the source has changed or gone. A PDF's snapshot is kept as soon as the file arrives, for its first reading to show; a reading that fails removes it, unless a document read from the same bytes keeps it.

The pictures of a source are kept with its document: those of a web page's figures, as its site served them, and those drawn from a PDF's pages. A picture is kept once, under the hash of its bytes, however many documents show it, and is served only to a reader whose library holds one of them. The text view (文本) shows the kept picture, in its place and at its size before it arrives. A figure whose picture is not kept shows the picture at its address on the web, asked for without a referrer, and a figure with neither shows only its words, if it has any.

infonium compresses what it sends, but for PDFs, pictures and recordings, whose bytes are compressed already, and names the version of each answer to a GET: a kept file by the tag of the file, any other answer by a hash of its bytes. A browser that asks again for an answer it holds is told, without the body, when its version is still the current one. So a refresh of the reader sends a source, its document and its translation again only once they have changed, and a picture, whose bytes never change, is kept by the browser for a year without asking.

Highlights and notes are kept in the library's file. Each holds passages, and a passage is an anchor for each block it runs through, all in one document. A highlight is one passage with a comment of up to 10,000 characters; marking the same passage again gives the same highlight. A note is a title of up to 200 characters and up to 500,000 characters of Markdown. A link in the Markdown whose address is `passage:` and an id names one of the passages kept beside the note, each with the source it lies in. A note belongs to the library, not to one source, and its links may lead into any source. A highlight put into a note leaves its comment there as a copy in the note's Markdown: changing the comment afterwards leaves the note as it is, and editing the note leaves the comment.

A document's identity follows from its item, the program that read it, and its blocks with their places, but not from the pictures of its figures. A reading that gives the same blocks gives the same document and adds no revision; one that gives a document the item held before returns the item to that revision, under its number. Such a reading keeps the document and its snapshot as they were first kept, but gives each figure that has no picture the picture this reading found for it: a page kept before infonium kept pictures, or one whose picture failed to arrive, gains it when it is read again. A figure that has a picture keeps it. Any other reading is kept as the next revision, numbered from 1 in the order the revisions were first read. Every revision is kept with its document and its snapshot. A highlight and a note's passage stay anchored in the revision they were marked in.

Where a passage stands in the document its item holds now is worked out each time it is shown, and never stored. A passage is looked for by its letters: its characters as a citation's quote is compared, without white space, block after block, so that neither spaces nor the places where blocks are cut decide what is found. A passage of 40 letters or more, each character of a script written without spaces counting 3, is found by its letters alone where each revision holds it exactly once. Any other passage must have both of its sides borne out. A side is borne out by the 10 letters right beside it, which must stand beside the place again with one letter read otherwise at most and no digit changed, or by the edge of its block: the passage began or ended fewer than 10 letters from that edge, the letters between stand as they were, and the place lies as far from the edge of its own block. At least one side must be borne out by letters. Footnote marks are left out of these letters, and new blocks of up to 16 letters, such as the link to edit a heading that a later reading of a wiki puts after it, are passed over.

In a table, a place may run across no more edges of cells and rows than the passage was marked across, and a cell is told by the letters of its own row. A passage that began and ended at the edge of a word is found only where it does so again, and a number is never found inside a longer number. Where the new revision does not hold the passage's very letters so placed, the passage is looked for among letters a little unlike them: four in five of its letters must stand after the fewest changes, no digit may change, the letters found must not have stood elsewhere in the revision the passage was marked in, and its sides must be borne out as above. For a passage of 40 letters or more that its revision held once, one side is enough, borne out by letters or by the document's edge, where its own first and last 10 letters stand at the ends of the place; no such place is taken outside the passage's own place while that still stands between the blocks that stood before and after it. A passage over several blocks between which new blocks came to stand is found block by block, with at most 2 new blocks of up to 16 letters between two of its blocks. A passage found as its very letters, spaced as they were, is shown as it is; one found as letters a little unlike, or with two of its words now glued together, is shown with the words 文字与标记时略有不同.

Where the revision a passage was marked in held other copies of it, or the new revision holds its letters in several places, the letters around each place are compared with those that stood around the marked copy, 32 on each side and up to 256 where those are the same, and the passage is found only at a place that they tell from every other. Where nothing tells the places apart, the passage is lost. Every rule leans the same way: a passage shown at a wrong place is worse than a passage said to be lost, which still opens in the revision it was marked in.

In the text of a source and in a recording's lines, highlights are drawn through the browser's registry of highlights, over the words, and nothing is written into the page. A selection becomes a passage from its two ends, by the same walk over each block's text that resolves a citation. With only a translation on show, it takes whole every sentence or paragraph it touches; with both on show, an end that lies in a translation takes the whole of that sentence or paragraph. On a PDF's pages, highlights and selections follow the document's line boxes. Each line is cut at the gaps between its words, read off the page as it is drawn: a page that holds highlights is read when it is drawn, any other at the first press on it. A line whose gaps cannot be read is cut by the widths of its characters, and a line of a script written without spaces into equal shares. A table, a figure and a box that stands for several lines are taken whole. Where the engine broke a scan's line inside a word, the break is moved, by 15 characters at most, only where both lines it bounds then hold as many words as their print shows. In the kept page of a web article, the characters an element shows are matched with its block's text, apart from white space, case and the shapes of quotation marks and dashes, and that one correspondence places a citation's passage, draws the highlights and turns a selection into a passage. Text the page holds but does not show is left out of it.

A note is written in an editor built on [TipTap](https://tiptap.dev/), fetched when a note is first opened, which writes Markdown. A note is handed to infonium 800 ms after the last key, and at once when the reader leaves it, the item or the page. Each writing carries an edition, one after the edition infonium last showed, and infonium takes a writing only when its edition is later than the one it holds. A reader whose note was written elsewhere meanwhile is told so, and nothing more is sent until they choose: to replace that writing with their own (用这里的替换那边的), to keep their own as a new note (另存为新笔记), or to drop their own and read in the other (放弃这里的，读入那边的).

A document is translated into the language of digests by a model of its own, the one the reader chooses. infonium first shows that model passages from across the document. The model says which language the document is in and, if it needs translating, lists the names and terms that recur, each with the one translation to use. In a technical document, a term of art that the source writes in English stays in English, as people of the field say it: the name of a model, a method, a component, a tool, a format or a measure, in the singular. At its first use in the document the reader's word for it follows in brackets, as a Chinese translation gives a gloss, Fresnel lens（菲涅尔透镜）, and nowhere after. The requests are made apart and at once, so infonium finds the block of each listed term's first use in the source and names that passage to the request that holds it; a term the survey did not list is given its word where a request first uses it, so it may be given it again in a later request. The general vocabulary of science and computing, which has a settled word in the reader's language, is translated, and so is everything in a source that is not technical. The list gives a term that stays in English as itself, so that requests write it alike. infonium then translates the document a few blocks a request, eight requests at a time, and keeps each block as it comes; each request carries the listed terms that its passages use. A link, a style or a formula of a passage goes to the model as a pair of tags around its words and comes back as a mark on the words that translate them. A formula is not translated: infonium puts its own text back between its tags, whatever the reply holds there, since a model that copies LaTeX alters it. A reply is kept only if it answers each block with exactly that block's passages, holds no remnant of a tag, and keeps every formula's tags with no word of the sentence inside them. The sentences of a recording are also checked against their neighbours: an answer out of proportion with its source, or the same answer to different sentences, is not kept. A reply that fails any of these is asked for again, up to three times. A table is translated cell by cell into the same grid, and a recording sentence by sentence along its stretches of time. Listings and formulas stay as written.

GLM's endpoint speaks the Chat Completions API and holds a reply to JSON, but not to a schema. infonium tells a GLM model the form of the reply after its instructions, by its name and its JSON schema, and holds the reply to that form as it reads it, as it does every reply. A statement of a reply that comes without its citations is taken as one that cites nothing. A GLM model always reasons, as much as `INFONIUM_GLM_REASONING_EFFORTS` asks of it. Where a reply may call tools, as one about the library does, the first request of the reply leaves the model to the form it is told, and every later request holds it to JSON: held to JSON, glm-5.3-flash called no tool in three tries of three, and left free after its searches it answered in prose two times in three. Digests and translations are told to punctuate as the reader's language is written, since glm-5.3-flash reasoning at length wrote commas and colons of Latin script between Chinese words.

GLM's endpoint limits how often an account may ask, over all its models, and refuses a request beyond that with 429 (code 1302). So every request to it, of translations, digests and answers alike, keeps one pace: it is sent at least 60 / `INFONIUM_GLM_REQUESTS_PER_MINUTE` seconds after the one before, the openai client's own second try among them. After a refusal with 429 no request is sent for 5 seconds; the pause doubles, up to a minute, while requests sent after it began are refused too, and is over with the first that is not. A request that waits for its turn is not sent if its reader leaves meanwhile.

The reader draws a PDF's translation itself, over the page. A block's lines that follow one another in one column are one area, and its translation is parted between its areas by the share of the source's text each holds. Body text, paragraphs and list items, is set at the size of the document's paragraphs (of its list items where it has no paragraph, of all its text where it has neither) wherever its lines stand as far apart as theirs, measured from the tops, the middles and the feet of one line to the next and taken as the median of them all; where they stand clearly closer, as in a footnote, or clearly further apart with larger letters, the size follows in proportion. A heading is set at the size its lines show, and every block in the colour of its source's ink.

A page's columns are told by the runs of three lines or more of all its blocks, translated or not. The areas of one column that follow one another down the page, on paper of one colour, with nothing drawn between their lines or beside them within the column, are one flow, and its areas are set one after another from its top, each from its own left side to the column's right, with the gaps their rooms leave on the page. An area with something drawn beside it, as a paragraph inside a frame, is a flow of its own within its own sides. An area whose lines lie for the most part within a line of another is a piece the engine cut out of that line, and stands in that line's flow as a block of its own, before or after the other's block as the document orders them.

The colour of the paper is laid across the column over the flow's room, from the top of its first area's room, or below what is drawn there above its lines, as the rule above a footnote, to the foot of its last one's, or short of what is drawn there under its lines, or of another block's lines that begin within it; the flow's own lines past that foot are covered by paper of their own, and the paper's edge, which covers the letters reaching past the boxes of their lines, is left off where something is drawn closer. What is drawn is read off the page as it is drawn; the letters of the lines beside a strip reach into it by a quarter of the body's size, and as far as their ink runs on unbroken, up to half of it, where the boxes of the lines leave descenders out: a run of drawn pixels twice the body's size long is a rule or the side of a frame, and anything more than specks beyond the reach of the letters around is a formula, a figure or a page number.

A page takes one leading for its body text, from 1.25 to 1.6 for a translation into a script written without spaces, as Chinese, and from 1.15 to the source's own for any other: of its flows that fit at the narrowest, the widest at which those that fit hold nine tenths of their letters (a flow's letters are the letters and digits of its translation, each formula counting as one), so that a few tight flows do not hold the rest back. A flow that fits at the narrowest but not at the page's leading takes the widest at which it fits. A heading is led in proportion to its own leading in the source. Beyond its foot a flow goes only over blank paper, a quarter of the body's size clear of what is drawn and of the next block's lines, with paper laid under its text: by a quarter of the body's size to fit, and where it does not fit at the narrowest leading by up to two lines; then it is set at 0.95, 0.9, 0.85 or 0.8 of its size, and at 0.8 is cut, with a mark that shows the rest when pressed, over the pages that follow where it runs past its own and under the reader's head. The room a flow leaves above its foot goes to the gaps between its blocks, at most half a line each and a line and a quarter before a heading, and the rest stays at its foot.

An area is set flush left where justifying it would widen the gaps of a line other than its last, its characters of Chinese, Japanese and Korean and their punctuation and its spaces, by more than 0.15 of the type size each; a line is what shows letters, digits, marks or a formula, and a raised or lowered letter or a formula belongs to the line it stands on.

A page is set when it is drawn, when a block of it arrives, when its fonts arrive, and once its width has stood still for 300 ms; a translation read again with the same blocks leaves it as it is, and while it is set again each flow keeps its setting, the keyboard's focus and whether it shows its source. In 对照 without room for two pages, a press on a flow shows the source under all of it until it is pressed again. A formula is set from its LaTeX by [KaTeX](https://katex.org/), here as in the text.

For a web article the reader can also show the kept page itself. infonium serves it with headers that let none of its scripts, plugins or frames run and send none of its forms. It removes from the page what would run, makes its links and forms open nothing by themselves, and marks the element that holds each block's text, and each cell of a table, so that the reader can put a translation in its place. A link that is clicked is opened by the reader, in a new tab.

A question about a source goes to the model that writes digests with the source's whole document and the earlier questions with their answers. A question about the library goes with an outline of the library and two tools that change nothing: one searches the library's blocks, the other reads a run of a source's blocks. Everything of a source reaches the model as JSON, so that a source's text cannot pass for anything but itself. The model answers with statements whose citations name a block and a quote. infonium keeps the citations it finds, as it does for a digest, and counts on each statement those it could not find.

The blocks of every document the library holds are indexed in the library's file, by their words and, through an embedding engine, by their meaning. A block is given to the engine under the title of its item. A search gives first the blocks that hold every word of it, then the blocks nearest to it in meaning and the blocks without a vector yet that hold some of its words, and says of each how it was found.

## Limits

### Web pages

- A site that turns programs away is not read. When it answers with anything but success, the reading fails with that status. When it answers with a check for a person in place of the page, the reading fails with the site's name, as long as the check is one that infonium knows; a check it does not know may be read as the page.
- A page of fewer than 200 words is taken for a check when its title is one that guards give their checks, or when the address it ends at names a captcha and carries another address to go on to, so a short page that happens to look like that is refused.
- A page whose HTML holds 200 words or more, menus and links included, is read from its HTML and never opened in a browser.
- A page of fewer than 200 words waits while it is opened in a browser, 90 seconds at most: the three such pages of a survey of 30 sites took 5 to 26 seconds.
- Of the HTML and the page the browser builds, the one with more words is kept, even when those words are menus and links.
- When the browser is missing or cannot render a page, the page is read from its HTML alone, and one with no text there fails with the browser's reason.
- A page whose HTML only asks for JavaScript may then be read as that one line.
- A formula in a web page is read as the characters the page shows for it, without their places: 1/k set as a fraction came out as 1k, in the document and in everything written from it.
- A page in the browser may take 3 GiB of memory, counted over the browser and the programs that drive it, and 1 GiB of disk. It may pass the memory bound by about 1.5 GiB, and the disk bound by what it writes in about 2 seconds, before it is ended.
- Nothing but its 90 seconds bounds the processor time a page takes in the browser, on every core the browser uses, and watching it costs infonium about an eighth of a core.
- A page in the browser reaches only public addresses, not this machine, a network one of its interfaces stands in, or the ranges set aside for special uses, but it can have this machine's resolver look up any name.
- Of public addresses, infonium cannot tell those that lead back into your own networks, so a page still reaches your router's public address and the ports it forwards, a company or campus network with public addresses, the networks a VPN routes to, a network's own NAT64 prefix, and services that trust this machine's address.
- A rendering does not go through a proxy set with `HTTP_PROXY` or `HTTPS_PROXY`, so where every connection must take such a proxy, a page that scripts build cannot be rendered and is read from its HTML alone; this comes from reading the code and has not been tried.
- If infonium is killed while a page is in the browser, the browser is ended and its folders are removed within half a second. If the program that drives the browser is killed with it, the browser's work folder stays until infonium starts again, and a small folder stays in the system's temporary folder.
- Each time an address of this machine changes, as when a container starts or stops beside it, the browser breaks off what a page is loading, and the page is opened anew once a second passes without a change, in at most five attempts, each begun within its first 25.5 seconds.
- When the network changed through every attempt, the fullest attempt is kept, which may hold only part of the page, and when neither it nor the page's HTML holds text, the reading fails saying that the network changed.
- The kept page (原件) is shown without its scripts, so it lacks what they build or show: menus, sections that a script unfolds, pictures that a script loads, and every style of a page that loads its stylesheet by script.
- The kept page asks its site for its stylesheets, pictures, fonts and media each time it is shown, its pictures too where infonium keeps them for 文本, so a page whose site has changed or gone is shown without them. The site learns infonium's address from some of these requests: over 51 pages, 83 requests named it, as the origin of a font request or as the referrer of what the page's own CSS loads.
- Some of those requests fail because the page is shown from infonium's address and sends no referrer: fonts that a site serves only to its own pages, and everything from a site that asks for its own referrer (on sspai.com, every stylesheet and article picture).
- Of the blocks of a web page that are not figures, 87% have an element of their own in the kept page (4,210 of 4,858 over 51 pages), and 22 of 35 tables. The others are shown there as the page has them, untranslated, a citation of one sends the reader to 文本, and the line above the page says how many they are. A page that parts its paragraphs by line breaks inside one element has many: 108 of the 274 blocks of an essay on paulgraham.com.
- A citation is marked in the kept page by its words where the page shows them as the block's text, white space and the quotation marks of an inline quotation aside. Elsewhere the whole paragraph is marked, and in a table its first cell.
- A link in the kept page opens in a new tab, and one to a place in the page scrolls there. A link to infonium itself, to anything but a web address, or, in a saved page with no web address of its own, to a relative address, does nothing.
- When the kept page cannot be fetched, or something other than the item's page comes back, 原件 says so and shows nothing of it; 文本 still has the whole text.
- In one column, where fewer than 160 pixels of the window are left for the kept page, it keeps 160 pixels and its foot lies under the column beside the reader.
- A page's pictures are downloaded when it is read: those of its figures, each address once, the first 200 the page shows. A picture is asked for as a browser that shows the page asks for it, by the user agent the page was fetched by, with the page's address as its referrer where it is of the page's own origin, the page's origin alone where it is of another, and no referrer from an https page to an http address; an address a redirect leads to is given its referrer by the same rule. The pictures of an uploaded page are asked for without a referrer.
- A picture is downloaded only from a public address, judged as a page in the browser's connections are: its host's name is looked up once, every address the name has must be public, and the connection is made to one of those. The downloads so reach what a page in the browser reaches, and each of up to 5 redirects is judged again. The downloads do not go through a proxy set with `HTTP_PROXY` or `HTTPS_PROXY`.
- A picture may hold 25 MB, and the pictures of a page 300 MB together. A picture may take 60 seconds, and the pictures of a page 120 seconds together, 6 at a time; every wait on a server ends by then but two: looking up the name of a picture's host, and making the connection, which takes 15 seconds at most. A lookup takes as long as this machine's resolver takes, so a resolver that does not answer holds a picture past its time until the lookup gives up. A server may be silent for 20 seconds. A picture that passes a limit, fails or is refused keeps its address, and 文本 shows it from its site; the reading goes on without it.
- A picture is kept as it was served, when Pillow, reading its header, finds a JPEG, PNG, GIF, WebP or AVIF picture. Its size is kept as a browser draws it, a JPEG turned as its Exif orientation says; the orientation of another kind is not read. A picture is asked for as its bytes are, and one served compressed all the same, with a `Content-Encoding` other than `identity`, keeps its address: no answer is decoded. A drawing in SVG keeps its address, and so does a picture shorter than 96 pixels on both sides, as an icon, an avatar or a spacer is, and one that Pillow takes for a decompression bomb, of more than 89,478,485 pixels. Of three articles tried, on en.wikipedia.org, bbc.com and sspai.com, 43 of 48 figures kept their pictures, 6.1 MB in all, those of sspai.com only for the referrer; the 5 left at their addresses were logos of Wikipedia's sister projects, 20 to 54 pixels across.
- A page read before infonium kept pictures shows its figures from their sites until it is read again; read again, it keeps its pictures, though its text has not changed.

### PDFs

- A PDF without a usable text layer, a scan above all, is read as pictures of its pages. The words of a paragraph or a heading are then recognised together and spread over its lines by their lengths, so a citation may glow over a neighbouring line as well, or miss a line that holds only a word of the quote. A caption, a footnote or a reference glows whole. The engine chooses one way of reading for a whole file: scanned pages in a file that has a text layer elsewhere are read line by line, which glues and splits more words.
- A region that MinerU takes for a picture or a chart, as a photograph, a drawing, a diagram, a plot or a screenshot, is a figure among the blocks, where it stands beside its caption and notes, which stay paragraphs of their own. Its picture is the region drawn alone from its page as the PDF shows it, at 144 dpi, and kept as WebP: the 9 pictures of each of two papers of 20 and 29 pages took 277 KB and 137 KB. A region whose picture would hold more than 89.5 million pixels, as one of about 4,730 points both ways or more does, is drawn at fewer dots to the inch, so that it holds no more: as many as Pillow opens without taking a picture for a decompression bomb, and as many as a web page's picture may hold. A region longer than 8191 points, about 2.9 metres, is drawn at fewer dots to the inch as well, so that its longer side keeps within the 16,383 pixels WebP holds. A picture that cannot be drawn, as one whose pixels want more memory than there is, is left out with its figure, and the log says which. MinerU's box of a region may end inside the drawing: in those papers 5 of the 18 ended 0.5 to 2 points short, through a frame or a turned axis label. So a picture takes in up to 2 points past each side of the box, but never more than half the way to another region MinerU found nearby, nor into a region that overlaps it, and with that all 18 came out whole. A drawing that reaches further, as the shadow of a picture on a scan, is still cut. A picture under 96 pixels both ways, about 17 mm on the page, is taken for an icon or a mark and left out, while its caption stays. The words inside a picture or a chart are not read, and are not cited, searched or translated: they are in its picture alone, unless MinerU finds them as a region of their own, as it found the title printed at the top of one sample's chart, which is then both a paragraph and part of the picture. A PDF in which MinerU finds nothing but pictures is refused, as holding no readable text. MinerU also takes some things for pictures that are not: a stamp, a row of ornaments, a large first letter, which may then be missing from its paragraph, and a box of code, whose code is then not read.
- A code listing is read as a whole in every PDF, and each of its rows is taken as an even slice of its box.
- A PDF with a text layer whose lines are written downwards is read badly: in the one page tried, halves of lines came out interleaved, characters were lost, and nearly all the text was marked as superscript or subscript. The same page as a scan was read well.
- A font may say that its letters reach less far above and below the line than they do: pdfTeX embeds XCharter saying its letters reach no higher than the x-height and not below the baseline. Read so, MinerU takes plain letters for raised or lowered ones and loses letters that reach below the line. In a paper of 20 pages, 5,087 runs of letters and stops, 13,467 characters in all, were marked as subscripts or superscripts, and "superintelligence" came out as "su erintelli ence". MinerU is therefore given a copy of such a PDF in which each such font says how far its Latin letters reach, as measured from them. The paper then came out with only its 7 footnote marks raised, and with the letters below the line that it had lost. Of 14 papers from arXiv, one other was given a copy, for fonts in its figures that say their letters reach neither up nor down, and it read the same. A font is measured by its Latin letters alone: in the samples drawn for infonium, letters in Bengali, Khmer and Tibetan are still taken for raised or lowered ones. A font that the PDF draws by procedures of its own, as matplotlib draws the text of a figure, and a font whose letters hang lower below the line than half their size, as a mathematical font's large operators do, are left as they are.
- MinerU takes either half of a character beyond the first 65,536 of Unicode for a broken character and writes � for it, and the mathematical fonts of TeX and of Word give their letters as such characters, those of the mathematical alphabets. In a paper of 20 pages, the 11 such letters that MinerU read as words of a sentence, as the 𝑁 of "𝑁 is the total number of action tokens", came out as �. MinerU is therefore given a copy of such a PDF in which each font gives such a letter as the plain letter it is a form of (N, α), and the paper then came out with none. Two other papers, whose text layers hold 852 and 13 such letters, all in formulas that MinerU reads by their LaTeX, read the same with the copy. A character beyond the first 65,536 that is a form of no other, as an emoji, still comes out as �.
- In a line that the PDF draws slanted, letters that reach below the line may be read as subscripts: in a paper whose e-mail addresses are an upright face slanted by about 9.5°, an address at google.com came out with both of its g's set low. MinerU measures by its ink every character that does not stand at exactly no angle, and to PDFium a slanted character stands at an angle.
- A scan of typewritten pages with wide line spacing was read a line a block: 101 of its 108 paragraphs were single lines. Such a document is translated a line at a time, which reads poorly.
- On a page that a PDF stores turned, a word may come out split in two when the file's own figures for the turn are not exact, as pypdf's `Transformation().rotate` writes them.
- How well a page read as pictures comes out depends on its writing, and that has been tried on no more than a paragraph of each. Latin, Cyrillic and Greek writing, Chinese and Japanese came out right or nearly, and Korean, Arabic, Thai, Hindi, Tamil, Bengali and Tibetan with a few wrong characters. In writing the engine does not know, Hebrew, Georgian, Armenian, Amharic and Khmer among what was tried, it writes words that are not on the page, and may take so long that the reading is given up.
- MinerU's models want a GPU. On the machine infonium was built on, a PDF of up to ten pages took about 4 to 5.5 GB of its memory, and a scan of 40 pages about 10 GB.
- MinerU reaches a server of its own on 127.0.0.1 through a proxy set with `HTTP_PROXY`, so where one is set, `NO_PROXY` must name 127.0.0.1. Behind a proxy that refused connections, a PDF failed once MinerU had waited for its server as long as `MINERU_LOCAL_API_STARTUP_TIMEOUT_SECONDS` allows (300 seconds unless set; 30 in the run), and with 127.0.0.1 named in `NO_PROXY` it was read in 16 seconds. That `ALL_PROXY` and a lowercase `http_proxy` do the same comes from reading the code.
- A link the translation does not carry cannot be pressed in 译文, as the translation covers it; in 原文 and in the source of 对照 it can. A link is found in the translation by its words: in the two papers tried, 173 of the 181 links over translated paragraphs of one were carried, and 295 of 339 in the other, whose letters were misread, the rest lying over words the source lost or the translation rewrote or left out. Where the block says a link's words more than once and not always as that link, the translation must say them as often, and the link goes to the saying at its place among them; a translation that changes their order moves the link with it.
- A link of the web opens only an http or https address, and not infonium's own. A mail address, a file and a script do nothing. The text (文本) of a PDF shows no links.
- Pages zoomed wider than the column scroll sideways by a touchpad, or by the wheel with Shift held; their own scroll bar stands under the last page. While they do, a cut flow of the last page shown whole is cut off where it runs past the foot of the pages. A page is drawn at no more than 2²⁵ pixels, as pdf.js's own viewer draws it, so at the largest zooms on a sharp screen its print is stretched a little. Once a zoom has settled, a page zoomed in past the drawing it has is drawn again, and a translation over it is read again from the new drawing and set again: in headless Chrome, which draws without the graphics card, at a device pixel ratio of 2, the longest pause this made was 0.12 s at 150% and 0.2 to 0.3 s at 300%. Ctrl with + or − still zooms the whole window. The zoom was tried in Chrome only.

### Recordings

- Of a video only the sound is kept. The reader plays it and does not show the picture.
- A WAV file of Vorbis sound, or of AAC sound without ADTS frame headers, is refused, since FFmpeg reads only part of such sound. FFmpeg writes both kinds when told to keep the sound of an Ogg or M4A file in a WAV. An M4A, an Ogg or an uncompressed WAV of the same recording is read.
- Speech is recognised in pieces of up to 16 seconds. A citation in a recognised recording plays from the start of the piece that holds the quote. A word at the edge of a piece is lost now and then, and each piece is punctuated by itself.
- The default speech model is less accurate in Chinese than in English.

### Digests

- A digest of gpt-6.1-terra took 41 to 116 seconds, 77 at the median, over 64 digests written through the relay infonium was tried with, which took 20 seconds to begin each answer. One of glm-5.3-flash took 12 to 43 seconds. A 快讯 of gpt-6.1-terra took 12 to 47 seconds, 31 at the median, over 16; one of gpt-6-luna 13 to 48, and one of GLM's models 4 to 30, 13 at the median.
- Every style sends the whole document with nearly the same instructions, so a digest's input depends on the source rather than the style: about 2,400 tokens for a news article of 33 blocks, 8,300 for a paper of 114 blocks, 11,300 for a Wikipedia article of 199 blocks and 13,500 for a radio transcript of 211 blocks. Its output was 1,000 to 3,600 tokens, the most for 章节速览, and 180 to 1,100 for a 快讯.
- The model does not set a word in bold in every statement: with the rules as they stand, 11% of the statements of 12 digests of gpt-6.1-terra had none, and 13% of those of 6 digests of glm-5.3-flash; of the statements of 48 flashes (快讯) of the four models, 11 of 141. 导读 says in its first sentence what kind of source it is, and may set that word in bold.
- A 快讯 holds three statements nearly always, however short its source: 45 of 48 did and the other three two, and a press release of three paragraphs got three in 13 of 14. Its statements ran 15 to 66 characters, counting Chinese characters, letters and digits, 33 at the median, and a whole 快讯 53 to 150.
- The first statement of a 快讯 gave the source's main news or finding in 47 of 48; glm-5.3 led the Chain of Density paper with its method. The BBC's "US regulators" stayed 美国监管机构 in the first statement of 3 of 4 flashes, which named the FCC in the next, and a press release's two agencies were named in all 14. Of Paul Graham's essay, whose text does not name its author, 13 of 18 flashes said 作者 (the author) and 4 made AI the subject; glm-5.3-flash named him once from outside the source, in 1 of the 6 flashes of GLM's models.
- Through the relay infonium was tried with, a model writes a section's title after its statements, and there it at times writes white space without end: the relay ends the reply after about 15 minutes, and the writing fails. The rules of 快讯 hold this back by saying that the title follows the statements. Of an essay of 16 blocks and a press release of 8, gpt-6.1-terra and gpt-6-luna did not end 15 of 55 flashes while the rules said nothing of where the title goes, empty or 快讯; since they say it, they ended all 54, and the other 44 flashes of every model and source ended too. Nothing in infonium ends such a reply yet, nor a digest's request when its reader leaves. The other styles also write their titles after their statements, and none has run away in the runs so far.
- A section now and then holds one statement more than its style allows: four caveats in two of the eight briefs of gpt-6.1-terra, and an answer of four statements in a 问答 of glm-5.3-flash.
- A section's title is the model's words for it in the language of digests, so titles vary from one digest to the next: 核心结论 or 底线 for the bottom line of a 简报, and 但是, 不过 or 需要注意 for its caveats. A 快讯 was titled 快讯 in each of 48.
- A citation is lost now and then, as in the chat: 8 beside 523 found in the 24 digests of one round, and 1 beside 195 in 48 flashes. gpt-6.1-terra at times writes the full stops inside an English quote as 。 (8 of 59 quotes seen in 20 of its replies), and a quote whose sentences it parts so is not found.
- 论文精读 of a source that is no paper fills its method and results with what comes nearest: the plans of a news story became its method.
- A word in bold that begins or ends with a mark or a symbol, such as 42% or a term in quotation marks, goes into a note as plain words, since Markdown would not hold it bold wherever it stands.
- Two pages that write the same style of one source at once ask the model twice, and the digest written last is kept; the views of one page share one writing.
- The style chosen last and the custom instructions written last are remembered by the browser they were chosen in: another browser starts without them.

### Translation

- Whether a document is in the language of digests already is judged by the translating model from up to 40,000 characters spread over it, by the language most of that sample is in. A document mostly in the reader's language is not translated, and its passages in other languages stay as written, unless the reader asks for it all the same (仍然翻译). A document mostly in another language is translated whole, and its passages already in the reader's language go to the model as well.
- A long document takes minutes. The 20,840 words of a Wikipedia article took 288 seconds, and a paper of 15 pages 37,000 input and 20,000 output tokens. The first blocks appear after one to two and a half minutes, most of it the survey, which lists each term with its forms and takes the longer the more it lists: on a busy day the survey of that paper took 53 to 110 seconds in nine runs, whether it read 10,000 or 39,000 characters of the paper, and the paper's first block came after 146 seconds.
- The translating model decides once for a document how its recurring names and terms are translated, and a wrong choice repeats wherever the term stands: in one Japanese article a shark was misnamed in every block that named it.
- In a technical source, which terms stay in English is the translating model's judgment, and on the borderline it differs from one translation to the next, though each keeps one way throughout: of the paper tried, one translation kept query, key and value in English and a later one translated them. A sentence can still keep a general word in English, such as state-of-the-art, or a plural, such as positional encodings. A term the source marks, as an emphasised word, can lose its space to the Chinese beside it: 3 of 145 places in the Rust chapter tried.
- A heading made only of terms of art, such as Scaled Dot-Product Attention, stays as written, and on a PDF's page it is set over the source's lines like any other block. In the paper tried one heading, Why Self-Attention, stayed in English whole.
- A translation made before this style keeps its terms translated until 全部重新翻译 is pressed; a block translated again by itself follows the terms of the translation it belongs to.
- A digest and a translation are written by different models and may give one name two translations: 吉普夫定律 in a digest, 齐普夫定律 in the translation of the same article.
- The translating model can misplace words: in a paper it swapped two formulas in one sentence. One block can be translated again by the model that writes digests.
- A link or a style is kept only where the model puts its tags back around the words that translate it. In the articles tried, 97 to 100 percent of links were kept, after asking again for any passage that lost half its marks or more.
- A block whose replies hold what is left of a tag, lose a formula's tags, put words of the sentence inside them or, in a recording, answer a sentence with the words of another is asked for again. After three attempts it stays in its source's language, and the translation shows as stopped, with how many of its blocks are translated and why the last failed. Asking for the translation again goes on with those blocks, and each of them can be translated by itself, by the model that writes digests.
- A citation leads to the sentences of a translation that stand for its own only as far as the translation keeps the source's sentences. In the two papers tried, 357 of 429 paragraphs and list items had as many sentences in Chinese as in English, and for each of the 45 citations of their digests the pairing found the sentence or the table row that translates the quote. Where a translation joins two sentences, the glow takes in both; where it splits or joins them unevenly, it may take in a neighbouring sentence.
- Cells of a table that hold only numbers are not translated and keep the source's way of writing numbers, such as a decimal comma.
- A line of a recording's translation ends at the end of a sentence, or at the first stretch that ends after 10 seconds. In the talks tried the longest line ran 16.5 seconds. A recording recognised without punctuation has no sentences, so every line of it is of the second kind.
- On a PDF's pages, a flow of translation that does not fit its room at the narrowest leading takes blank paper under it, then is set smaller, down to 0.8 of its size, and beyond that cut, with a mark that shows the rest when pressed. Two papers translated into Chinese were measured, with pages 992 px wide: a two-column paper of 29 pages, whose 327 areas made 70 flows, and a one-column paper of 20 pages, whose 199 areas made 38 flows. In the first, a flow of nine areas and one of a single area were set at 0.95; in the second, three flows. None was cut. 72 areas of the first and 36 of the second were set flush left. The pages of the first took leadings from 1.25 to 1.6, 18 of its 28 translated pages 1.6, and on 3 of them 4 flows that fit only more tightly took a leading of their own, from 1.35 to 1.55; those of the second took from 1.25 to 1.6, 11 of its 20 pages 1.35 or less, as its lines are taller with letters wrongly read as raised or lowered, and none of its flows took a leading of its own.
- A few words the engine read apart from the line they stand in, as "43rd" from a footnote's line, are set on a line of their own before or after the paragraph they were cut from, which then lacks them.
- On a PDF's pages in 对照, a translated paragraph does not stand level with its source: the blocks of a flow follow one another from its top, so a block below a shorter translation stands higher than its source's lines.
- The leading of a PDF's translation may differ from page to page, since each page takes the widest that nine tenths of its text allow, and it depends on the page's width, as a narrower page breaks its lines at other places: of the second paper's 20 pages, 10 took a leading 0.05 or 0.1 tighter at 712 px wide than at 992, and of the first paper's 29 pages one did, by 0.15. Pages on show may therefore be set at another leading once a change of width has settled, and their text then flows anew once. It depends on the screen as well, as a screen of another device pixel ratio places letters a fraction of a pixel apart and may break a line elsewhere: between ratios 1 and 2, the first paper's pages took the same leading at both widths, and of the second paper's 20 pages one took another at 992 px wide (1.30 for 1.45) and seven at 712 px (six by 0.05, one by 0.20). The flows themselves were the same in every case.
- A block whose lines run on from one column into the next, or from one page to the next, is parted between them by the share of its source's text each holds, not by the room each has: one part may be set smaller while the other leaves paper blank.
- Whether the paper between two blocks is blank is read off the drawn page. On a scanned page, specks and uneven paper between paragraphs may end a flow, so that its areas are set apart; this comes from reading the code and has not been tried.
- A very pale ground, as the light grey panel of a figure, is taken for blank paper, so a flow that runs past its room may lay its paper over it. On the first page of the one-column paper, translated 2.5 times as long and with pages 992 px wide, the abstract's paper covers the top 15 px of the figure's panel under it, though none of the figure's ink.
- On a PDF's pages, tables, listings and blocks that are one formula stay as the page has them; a table's translation is read under 文本. Small leftovers of the source's letters may show between the translated areas of a dense or scanned page.
- A formula that KaTeX cannot set is shown as its LaTeX, and so is one that writes a command to load an address, to set a class, an id, a style or an attribute, to define a command or to write to the console. MinerU writes a subscript or a superscript inside `\text{…}` at times, which neither LaTeX nor KaTeX sets: a formula KaTeX refuses as written is tried once more with each such group (`\text`, `\textbf`, `\textit` and their kin) closed before its first script, and the rest of the group after it. Of two papers, all 100 formulas of one and all 59 of the other were set, one of these after that second try. What a formula draws outside its own box, as with a negative space, is cut, and so is a formula's height beyond 40 times the size of its text. A selection across a formula copies the characters KaTeX draws, each script on a line of its own, not its LaTeX: a wide hat is lost, and a subscript may bring a zero-width space with it.
- With a translation on show, a citation marks the translated block, or the lines of a recording, that hold the quote, not the quoted words themselves.
- The switch keeps its place from one source to the next, so a source opened while it stands on 译文 or 对照 is translated without another word from the reader.
- If infonium is killed during a translation, the requests in flight are made again when it starts; blocks already kept are not. A document whose item is read again meanwhile is not translated further.
- A translation that goes on with another model keeps the blocks translated before; only 全部重新翻译 makes them all with the model chosen.
- How much a GLM model reasons decides most of what a request costs. Translating 13 blocks of 3,800 characters in one request, two runs a setting: glm-5.3-flash took 8 to 10 seconds and 1,100 to 1,270 output tokens at `none`, `minimal` and `low`, 11 to 14 seconds and 1,320 to 1,540 at `medium`, and 20 to 40 seconds and 2,600 to 4,800 at `high`, `xhigh`, `max` and by default; glm-5.3 took 9 to 15 seconds and 990 to 1,550 at `none` to `medium`, 40 to 151 seconds and 4,500 to 16,900 at `high`, `xhigh` and `max`, and 91 to 100 seconds and 10,500 to 11,100 by default. The translations themselves take about 1,000 of those tokens. glm-5.3 kept to the request's list of terms at every setting; glm-5.3-flash kept a term of art in English where the list translated it in every run below `medium`, in one of two at `medium`, and in none above.
- On a made-up article of 41 blocks and 13,300 characters, asked to reason little, glm-5.3-flash translated the whole in 11 to 14 seconds and glm-5.3 in 20, four requests at once, with no request refused and every link and style kept. glm-5.3-flash with the endpoint's default took 75 to 128 seconds on a technical article of 9 blocks.
- A term's word in brackets at its first use is the model's to give, and a name kept as written may be given its sound in Chinese, as in Quillmoor（奎尔摩尔）.
- GLM does not say how many requests an account may make: its Coding Plan's limit depends on the plan and changes with the load. On 2026-11-25 eight requests of glm-5.3-flash sent at once were refused with code 1302, where four at once had been answered the day before; at 8 requests a minute about four of them, of about 28 seconds each, are under way at once. A refusal still spends one of a request's three tries, so a translation refused for minutes on end stops. While a translation by a GLM model runs, a question asked of a GLM model waits for its turn among the translation's requests, with no precedence over them.
- The endpoint of GLM's Coding Plan, the default, is meant for the coding tools the plan names: Zhipu's FAQ says that called from elsewhere the plan's quota does not apply, and that a request may then be refused (error 1113) or charged to the account's balance. GLM's general endpoint, `https://open.bigmodel.cn/api/paas/v4`, is paid by use.

### The chat

- infonium checks that each quote of an answer stands in the block it names, not that the statement follows from it: one answer about a story added a sum that its cited passages do not hold.
- The model now and then translates a quote instead of copying it, once in five tries of one question. Such a citation is not found, and its statement is marked 出处找不到 although the source holds what it says.
- A GLM model often leaves the citations out of the statement that says the source does not tell, though the form of the reply requires them: in 7 of 21 answers of glm-5.3 and glm-5.3-flash to two questions that their papers do not answer. Such a statement is kept as one that cites nothing, as the rules ask.
- Every question about a source sends the source's whole document to the model again: about 9,000 input tokens a question for a Wikipedia article of 165 blocks. A document too long for the model cannot be asked about, just as it cannot be digested.
- An answer about a source begins to appear after 2.5 to 5 seconds, and after up to 18 seconds when the model reasons first.
- A question about the library takes 6 to 42 seconds and 4,000 to 53,000 input tokens with the embedding engine running, measured on two libraries of nine and ten sources, and up to 80,000 tokens without it, when the model searches longer. When the endpoint stalls in the middle of an answer, as it did once in ten, it takes longer: 99 seconds that time.
- For a question about the library the model may call its tools in at most 7 rounds of at most 4 calls each. Its last request offers no tools, so it answers with what it has found.
- A question in one language does not always reach a source in another: the model does not always search in every language of the library, and a search by meaning in English did not bring a Chinese passage on the same subject.
- The model is told the titles of at most 80 sources, each cut at 120 characters; it finds the others by searching.
- Earlier questions are given to the model again only as far as 12,000 characters of them go: the latest always, its answer cut where it is longer, and the model is told how many older ones are left out.
- A conversation answers one question at a time: a second question, or clearing the conversation, is refused while it answers. A reader who leaves before an answer is complete keeps nothing of it.
- The question being typed and an answer being written are held by the open page: reloading it keeps only the answers that infonium has kept.
- While the embedding engine is away, nothing on the page says that the library is being searched by words alone.
- A digest written before infonium counted the citations it could not find shows a statement that lost all of them as 无出处, like one that was given none.

### Highlights

- In the text, a press opens a highlight 250 ms later, so that a double click selects a word instead. A slow double click, its presses 300 ms apart or more, opens the highlight and selects the word as well.
- In the text, a highlight made with only a translation on show, or with an end in a translation, covers each piece of the source it touches whole: a sentence of a recording, a paragraph of anything else, and a table for one of its cells.
- The notebook was tried in Chrome only. Pressing a highlight in the text opens it only in a browser that tells the words under a point, Chrome from 128 on. Selecting in the text by keyboard needs the browser's caret browsing (F7 in Chrome).
- In a narrow window, when a selection ends at the window's foot, its actions stand above its last line and cover the line before it.
- On a scan, the ends of a highlight on the pages are read off the print where the reading engine's line breaks agree with it. Where the engine broke a line inside a word, a highlight may be drawn up to a word away from its words: 7 of 42 random highlights on seven test scans.
- On the lines whose gaps could be measured, of five scans and three PDFs with a text layer, a word's edge lies a median 0.03 to 0.06 of a character from the print. It lies more than two characters off at no edge of the three PDFs with a text layer, at 1.8% of edges at most on four of the scans, and at 13.7% on the fifth, whose blocks the engine split into lines of even length.
- A line whose print is not dark on light paper, such as light text on a dark page or grey text, is cut by the widths of its characters, which on scans puts a word's edge a median 0.9 to 2.6 characters off.
- A highlight on a page not yet read is first drawn by the widths of its characters, and moves once the page is read: in the case watched, 2 seconds later and by about two characters.
- In the one PDF tried whose text layer runs downwards, the engine's line places do not match its columns, and highlights are drawn away from their words.
- On a PDF's pages, select-all (Ctrl+A) hands over every block of the document, and 高亮 then marks all of it.
- In the kept page (原件), a highlight in a block that has no element of its own there is not drawn, and the line above the page says how many such highlights there are. Where an element shows a translation, or the highlight's words are not found in it, the element is tinted whole.
- In the kept page, words the page holds but does not show, such as text for screen readers, are never part of a highlight, and words it shows struck out inside a highlight are marked with it.
- A link in the kept page opens only on a press that moved 4 pixels or less, or by Enter. A drag that starts on a link selects its words, unless it starts on the left half of the link's first character, where the browser begins no selection. No link can be dragged out of the page, and links the page made unselectable can be selected.
- A highlight in the kept page keeps its light green on a dark page and in the dark theme.
- Marking by touch was tried only in emulation, not on a phone or with a pen. On a PDF's pages, a finger held still for 450 ms marks the whole block it rests on and does not select words. In the kept page, touch was not tried.
- A comment typed and not yet kept survives turning to another item, but is lost when the page is loaded again.
- A page open in a second tab shows a highlight made, changed or removed in the first once that tab is turned to, not before.
- In 对照 in the text, a highlight is drawn on the source's words, not on the translation beside them.
- A passage that runs through a formula is quoted in the notebook and in a note with the formula's LaTeX.
- 500 highlights on one item added 60 to 85 ms to opening it in Chrome, and about 0.5 seconds with the processor slowed four times.
- The notebook was not tried with a screen reader.

### Notes

- Editing a note changes what the editor cannot show: a table is dropped, a picture keeps only its alt text, and HTML becomes marks or text. When the note is opened, it says which of these, or footnotes, it holds.
- The first writing of a note puts some Markdown in another form of the same meaning, and from then on it stays as written: a `*` bullet becomes `-`, `_x_` becomes `*x*`, an underlined heading one marked by `#`, an indented listing a fenced one, a mark inside a word is escaped (`snake\_case`), and a bare address becomes a link.
- A link to a passage inside bold or italic loses that mark when the note is edited.
- Two writers saving the same note at the same moment, one of them as its page is hidden or closed, can lose the other's last change, and neither is told.
- When a writing reached infonium but its answer was lost, the note says it was written elsewhere though nothing was lost; 用这里的替换那边的 sets it right.
- A writing of more than 64 KiB cannot be sent as the page closes. The browser asks first; a reader who leaves all the same may lose what was typed last.
- When infonium refuses a passage of a writing, such as one pasted from elsewhere that its source does not hold, every link new in that writing stands as plain words, and the note names the refused one.
- The editor is fetched when a note is first opened: 481.54 kB of script, 153.01 kB gzipped.
- A note of 50 passages in 20 items of 2,000 blocks each opened in 157.5 ms the first time and in 8.0 ms once those documents were read. infonium keeps the 64 documents read last.
- A screen keyboard was tried only as a window of 390 by 420 pixels, and the editor was not tried with a screen reader.
- A comment written from right to left stands aligned to the left in a note, as every paragraph of a note does, though on its highlight it stands to the right.
- An address with Chinese text right after it, with no space between, takes that text into its link: `https://example.com/page中的说法。` links `https://example.com/page中的说法`. A Chinese full stop, comma or bracket ends it, and so does a closing quotation mark, ” or ’.
- Text joined to an address of this machine, with no space between, stays text, a web address in it included: `http://localhost/x”和www.example.com` links nothing.
- An address typed or pasted into a note is linked by the editor's own rule, not by the one above: a Chinese full stop or comma right after it is taken into the link (`见 www.example.net/x， 完` links `http://www.example.net/x，`, and `https://example.com/x。` keeps its `。`); an address glued to the word before it, as `参考www.example.org`, becomes a link only once the note is loaded again; and a paste that runs across a closing quotation mark, as `“https://example.com/q”和www.example.com/a*b。`, becomes one link.

### Reading a source again

- A passage whose words still stand may be said to be lost: most often a passage of few words whose neighbours changed, a cell of a changed table, or text that a recognition repeats. Of 100 passages of each of three Wikipedia articles read in two revisions, 2, 2 and 0 were. Of 100 passages of each of two PDFs of many scripts, read from their text layer and again as pictures, 7 and 18 were. Of 2,400 passages of twelve documents read again with their blocks cut and joined, 50 were; with misreadings added, 86; with 15% of their blocks also rewritten or removed, 97. Of 600 passages of each of four pairs made from the Rust and 東京都 Wikipedia articles, with blocks removed, rewritten and copied, 23 to 26 were.
- None of the passages above was shown at a place that does not hold its words. A wrong place is still met now and then in documents made up to say the same rows, sentences and formulas again and again with small changes: in 4 of 11 runs of the test that mixes such documents, one passage was found away from its own place, such as a cell of a removed row found in another row, or a passage across two blocks found a little unlike itself on a neighbouring sentence of the same form. Where identical rows or sentences stand side by side and one is removed, a passage of the removed one is shown in one that stayed.
- In the same made-up documents, a sentence that every section says, in a block of its own, is often lost when the section beside it was removed or rewritten, though its own block stands word for word.
- Where a paragraph was rewritten or removed and a new paragraph that says the same but for one word was put elsewhere, a mark on part of the first is often shown on the second, with the words that say it differs a little: in a test made for it, 72 of 166 and 55 of 146 passages whose paragraph was rewritten, and 83 of 180 and 77 of 176 whose paragraph was removed.
- A passage of 40 letters or more that each revision holds once is found wherever it stands: where its own paragraph was removed and the same words stand in another, it is shown there.
- A passage is lost where a digit of it other than a footnote mark's changed, however little else did, and where more than one letter in five changed: of 100 passages of a PDF in Hebrew, Georgian, Armenian, Amharic and Khmer, read again as pictures, 41 were lost because the recognition wrote other text.
- The notebook works out where each highlight stands each time it lists them. After a second reading of a made-up page of 2,001 blocks and about a million characters, a list of 50 highlights took 0.25 to 0.36 seconds, one of 300 took 1.3 seconds and one of 500 took 2.1 seconds. A highlight of a single common word costs more: with ten of them among the 50, the list took 0.7 seconds. One passage took 0.9 seconds at most, on a megabyte of text that says one row of a table ten thousand times.
- A passage that its document says more than 2,000 times is lost, once anything in the document changed, when its own copy comes after the 2,000th.
- Every revision is kept with its document and its snapshot, and none is ever removed.
- Where a new paragraph of a few words came to stand inside a passage that runs over several paragraphs, the passage is found with the new paragraph inside it.
- A passage that begins with a combining mark set after white space is lost even in a reading that changed nothing: 15 of 3,988 such made-up passages.
- An earlier revision is shown as its text only, without its pages, its kept page, its player or a translation, and nothing can be marked in it.
- An upload whose reading never succeeded cannot be read again; it has to be uploaded again.
- In a library kept before revisions, an item's earlier readings are numbered in the order they were last read, not first read.

### Finding passages in the library

- A block is found by its words at the first search after its reading settles, and by its meaning once it has a vector. The blocks read last get theirs first: a page read behind 9,500 waiting blocks had them within 3 seconds.
- A heading, and any other block of fewer than 3 words (a Chinese or Japanese character counting as one), is never found by meaning, only when it holds every word of the search: 29% of the blocks of a library of 8 Wikipedia pages.
- A block that shares with a search only a word that more than a tenth of the library's blocks hold, and more than ten of them, is not found by that word.
- A short block that says what longer blocks beside it say may rank below them: a five-word Finnish answer came 6th for a question in English and 11th for one in Chinese.
- A search by meaning prefers the language it is written in: for a question in English, a Chinese passage that answered it was not among the first fifty blocks.
- While the embedding engine is away (no answer, a timeout, 429 or 5xx), searches go by words alone, and blocks get their vectors within 10 seconds of its answering again. Any other error of the engine stops the search of the library until infonium starts again.
- When another writer holds the library's file past 30 seconds, the index waits a round and tries again. Any other fault of the index stops the search of the library until infonium starts again.
- Words are found in the form written, but a Korean word also finds the forms it starts (서울 finds 서울은). Chinese, Japanese, Thai, Lao, Burmese and Khmer are found by any two or more letters side by side.
- `engines/embedding` holds the engine to 4 processors. Over 9,500 waiting blocks it took 97 seconds, and searches meanwhile took 56 ms at the median.
- The first search after starting on a large library not yet indexed waits for its words: 12 seconds for 320,000 blocks. Over 100,000 blocks, a search took 29 ms at the median by words alone and 123 ms with vectors.

### In general

- The reason shown when a call of the model or of an engine fails gives the address the call went to and what was answered.
- An address is fetched for up to ten minutes and up to 2 GiB, and given up once its server sends nothing for 20 seconds. A video or podcast page is fetched for up to an hour, and its recording may run up to four hours and hold up to 2 GiB. What goes beyond is not read.
- The browser is given none of infonium's settings or keys, only `PATH`, the locale, the time zone and a home of its own.
- The other programs infonium starts are given none of them either, nor any variable named like a key, a token, a password or a secret: FFmpeg only `PATH`; the fetcher of video and podcast pages `PATH`, the locale, the proxies and yt-dlp's `YTDLP_` settings; MinerU `PATH`, the locale, the proxies, `HOME`, `XDG_CACHE_HOME`, `LD_LIBRARY_PATH`, its own `MINERU_` settings, the GPU's `CUDA_` settings and the `HF_`, `HUGGINGFACE_` and `MODELSCOPE_` settings of the hubs it fetches its models from. Only `file` is given all of infonium's environment: when infonium loads yt-dlp, once each time infonium starts and each time `--reload` restarts it, Python runs `file` on its own executable, and `file` reads that executable and its magic database and nothing else.
- infonium serves one person on one machine. It has no accounts and no authentication, so keep its ports off the network.

## Development

```bash
make check       # lint, types, and generated protocol files are current
make test        # the tests that need nothing outside the repository
make protocol    # regenerate protocol/openapi.json and web/src/protocol.d.ts
```

The HTTP protocol is defined by the models in `src/infonium`. `protocol/openapi.json` and `web/src/protocol.d.ts` are generated from them and never edited by hand.

`make test` runs the tests in `tests` without the network, a browser, MinerU, FFmpeg, a speech recogniser, an embedding engine or a model: stand-ins answer for the model and the embedding engine, and a test that opens a network socket or looks up a name fails. It takes about four minutes. Three tests of where a passage stands after a second reading are marked as failing at times, each with the limit it stands for. A test that needs one of these programs carries its name as a marker and runs when the marker is named; it is skipped when the program is not found the way infonium finds it:

```bash
uv run pytest -m chrome    # Chrome or Chromium, by INFONIUM_BROWSER
uv run pytest -m ffmpeg    # ffmpeg and ffprobe
uv run pytest -m mineru    # MinerU 3.4.2, by INFONIUM_PDF_ENGINE, on a GPU
```

The tests of the pace of requests to GLM's endpoint wait out real pauses and take about seven seconds. They carry the marker `pace`, are left out of `make test` as well, and are run after a change to the pace (`Pace` and the pauses in `src/infonium/model_connection.py`). On a busy machine one of them fails at times: its four requests sent at once do not all reach the stand-in before the first refusal begins the pause.

```bash
uv run pytest -m pace      # the pace of requests to GLM's endpoint
```
